## Overview

The Dodge Challenger arrived for the 1970 model year as Dodge's entry in the pony-car market,
built on Chrysler's new E-body platform, shared with the Plymouth Barracuda but with a longer
wheelbase. The R/T ("Road/Track") was the performance version. In 1970 Dodge sold a little over
18,000 Challenger R/Ts in hardtop, special edition (SE) and convertible form. The R/T SE added a
vinyl roof with a smaller rear window and upgraded interior trim. A white 1970 Challenger R/T
became a film icon in the 1971 movie "Vanishing Point".

## Engines and drivetrain

The R/T came standard with the 383 cubic inch "Magnum" V8 rated at 335 hp, which went into about
two thirds of the cars (12,281). Three big-block upgrades were offered:

- the 440 Magnum with a single four-barrel carburetor, 375 hp (3,840 cars);
- the 440 Six Pack with three two-barrel carburetors, 390 hp (2,035 cars);
- the 426 Hemi, 425 hp, the rarest and most valuable (356 cars).

Buyers could choose a four-speed manual, often with a pistol-grip shifter, or the three-speed
TorqueFlite automatic. The fifth character of the VIN identifies the engine: a "V" marks a
factory 440 Six Pack car, "U" the 440 four-barrel and "R" the Hemi.

## Identification and options

Check that the VIN, the fender tag and the engine stamping agree. R/T cars carry specific
badging, a performance hood and heavy-duty suspension. The 1970 Challenger was famous for its
"High Impact" paint colors, including Plum Crazy (purple), Sublime (green), Go Mango and Hemi
Orange. Options that matter to collectors include the shaker hood scoop, the Rallye instrument
cluster, the SE package and the convertible body. Many base Challengers have been cloned into
R/Ts or Hemi cars, so the paperwork and the VIN are essential.

## Known issues and red flags

E-body Chryslers rust in the rear quarter panels, trunk pan, rear window channel (especially
under vinyl roofs, which trap moisture), floor pans and the cowl. Check the frame rails and the
leaf-spring mounts. Big-block cars are front-heavy and were often driven hard: look for
worn front suspension, steering box play and overheating. A non-original engine in a 440 or
Hemi car destroys most of its premium. Six Pack carburetor setups need careful synchronization
and are often replaced by a single four-barrel; original Six Pack hardware adds value.

## What drives value

The engine is the first driver of value (Hemi, then 440 Six Pack, then 440 four-barrel, then
383), followed by numbers-matching originality, documentation, body style (convertible and SE
carry premiums), High Impact colors and the quality of the restoration. Condition grades used by
this dealer: 1 = concours, 2 = excellent driver-quality restoration, 3 = good driver,
4 = needs work, 5 = project car.
