## Overview

The Buick Grand National is the turbocharged, all-black version of the Buick Regal coupe,
built on General Motors' rear-wheel-drive G-body platform. In an era when V8 power had fallen
sharply, the Grand National embarrassed many V8 sports cars with a turbocharged V6, earning
the nickname "Darth Vader's car". 1987 was its final and most popular year: Buick built
20,193 Grand Nationals. Of those, 547 were converted into the limited-edition GNX.

## Engine and drivetrain

The Grand National uses a 3.8 liter (231 cubic inch) turbocharged V6 with sequential fuel
injection and an intercooler, rated at 245 hp and 355 lb-ft of torque. The large torque figure
at low rpm is what makes the car feel so strong from a standstill. Power goes to the rear wheels
through a four-speed automatic transmission; no manual gearbox was offered. The GNX version,
built with ASC/McLaren, was rated at 276 hp and 360 lb-ft and is widely believed to have been
underrated.

## Identification and options

1987 Grand Nationals were sold only in black. Look for the turbo V6, the Grand National
badging and the correct interior. Buick also sold a turbocharged Regal T-Type and a "Limited"
trim with the same engine, which are easier to confuse with a Grand National, and some
Regals have been dressed up as Grand Nationals. The GNX is easy to tell apart: unique fender
vents, special wheels, a different instrument panel and a numbered plaque. Options include
T-tops; cars without them are stiffer and often preferred by drag racers.

## Known issues and red flags

The turbo system is the main thing to inspect: oil leaks at the turbocharger, smoke at
start-up, cracked or loose intercooler and boost hoses, and signs of over-boosting by
previous owners (aftermarket chips, boost gauges, upgraded fuel pumps). Many cars were modified
for drag racing; an unmodified car is worth considerably more. Check for detonation damage
history, a tired original fuel pump, sagging headliners (very common on G-bodies), leaking
T-top seals and electrical issues in the dashboard. Rust is less of a problem than on 1960s
cars, but check the rear wheel wells and the lower doors.

## What drives value

Originality (stock engine management, untouched turbo setup), low mileage, documentation
such as the original window sticker, condition of the black paint and interior, and the absence
of drag-racing modifications. A GNX is a different market altogether. Condition grades used by
this dealer: 1 = concours, 2 = excellent, 3 = good driver, 4 = needs work, 5 = project car.
