## Overview

The Chevrolet Camaro Z/28 is the road-racing version of the first-generation Camaro, built
from 1967 to 1969. Chevrolet created it to homologate the Camaro for the SCCA Trans-Am series,
whose rules limited engine displacement to 5 liters (305 cubic inches). Z/28 was the regular
production option code, not a model name, which is why early cars wore few badges. Production
grew from a few hundred cars in 1967 to 20,302 in 1969, the best-known year. The 1969 model
year ran long, into late 1969, before the second-generation Camaro arrived in 1970.

## Engine and drivetrain

The heart of the Z/28 is the 302 cubic inch (4.9 L) small-block V8. Chevrolet combined the
4-inch bore of the 327 with the 3-inch stroke of the 283 to get under the 305 cubic inch limit.
It was advertised at 290 hp at 5,800 rpm and 290 lb-ft of torque at 4,200 rpm, a figure widely
considered underrated: the engine loves high rpm thanks to solid (mechanical) lifters, a
high-lift camshaft and a large Holley four-barrel carburetor. Every Z/28 came with a four-speed
manual transmission; no automatic was offered. Power front disc brakes were part of the
package in 1969, and a performance rear axle was standard. On a genuine 1969 car the engine
block carries the "DZ" suffix code that identifies the 302.

## Identification and options

A real 1969 Z/28 combines the 302 engine, the four-speed and the Z/28 equipment, usually with
painted rally stripes on the hood and trunk lid and Z/28 emblems. Popular options included the
cowl-induction hood, which draws cold air from the base of the windshield, Rally Sport hidden
headlights, and rear spoilers. Because the Z/28 package is so desirable, many ordinary Camaros
have been converted into "tributes" or clones. Documentation matters: original dealer invoice,
build documentation, the trim and cowl tags, and matching engine and transmission codes.

## Known issues and red flags

First-generation Camaros rust in predictable places: lower rear quarter panels, trunk floor,
the area under the rear window, floor pans and the cowl, which can leak water into the cabin.
Inspect the rear frame rails and subframe mounts. On the drivetrain, a non-original block (no
DZ code), a replacement four-speed or a changed rear axle lowers value sharply. High-revving
302s that were raced may show oil consumption or worn valvetrain parts; a compression and
leak-down test is a good investment. Reproduction body panels and stripes are good quality and
common, so originality must be proven with paperwork, not by eye.

## What drives value

Originality ("numbers-matching" engine, transmission and axle), documented history, factory
options (cowl-induction hood, Rally Sport package), desirable original colors such as Hugger
Orange, and the quality of any restoration. Condition grades used by this dealer:
1 = concours, better than new; 2 = excellent driver-quality restoration; 3 = good driver with
visible wear; 4 = needs work; 5 = project car.
